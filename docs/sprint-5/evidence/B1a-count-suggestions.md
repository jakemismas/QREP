Spike for issue #134 (plan ticket B1a). It measures whether QREP can suggest the square counts
of a confirmed field well enough for B7 to build, and records the go or no-go. The header
above is the run that produced every number here; `B1a-count-suggestions.json` holds each one
with its n, and the generated tables follow this reading.

## Decision

No count method and no autocorrelation tap seed ships in B7. 0.4.0 ships typed counts,
steppers, C3a's geometric tap-one-square seed (the field's extent over one tapped square's
width, which plan C3a ships whatever B1a finds) and the fit check, and scope does not change
(plan section 10, risk 2).

The rule (issue #134, criterion 6): a method ships when its top-3 recall on the in-scope dev
photos is at least 0.8 and the exp2 fit score rejects every wrong top-1 it produced. The plan
sets the 0.8 bar; Jake may change it. The fit rule is read as plan B3 states exp2: two scores,
the seam-to-centre ratio (high is good) and the median within-square chroma MAD (low is good),
each with one threshold set where every true count passes. The recall is joint: a trial is a
top-3 hit when the true (across, down) pair is among the first three pairs, because a
suggestion offers one pair of counts. Per-axis recall runs higher (fit, 2 px, phone,
proposed-a: 387 of 440 axes, 0.880) and does not change the decision: the global rule rejects
every wrong top-1 only on two tap rows at 0 px, whose per-axis top-3 is 0.227 (5 of 22) and
0.318 (7 of 22).

- No method clears both parts at any radius (0 to 8 px), on either staging cap (phone 1400 px,
  desktop 2000 px), against either proposal pass (proposed-a, proposed-b): Go is no-go in all
  100 groups.
- Only the two fit methods reach a top-3 recall of 0.8: at 0 px (9 of 11) and 2 px on both caps
  and both passes (best: fit, phone, proposed-a, 182 of 220, 0.827), and at 4 px on the desktop
  cap only (178 of 220, 0.809, on proposed-a and 179 of 220, 0.814, on proposed-b, for both fit
  methods). Every one of those rows fails the fit rule. At 2 px (phone, proposed-a) the two
  scores reject 26 of the fit method's 73 wrong top-1s: across that row's 220 true-count fits
  the MAD reaches 12.37 and the ratio falls to 1.21, so a block-count
  harmonic passes both thresholds.
- Two other readings of the fit rule cannot decide. Per trial, against the true count's two
  scores in the same trial, the rule rejects 4 of 4 wrong top-1s at 0 px for fit and
  fit_interior on both caps and both passes, with a top-3 of 9 of 11 (0.818, Wilson 0.523 to
  0.949), so that reading would ship both at 0 px; at 2 px (phone, proposed-a) it rejects 67
  of 73. But it scores each wrong top-1 against the true count, which production never knows:
  a check sees the photo and the suggested or confirmed count, not the truth. The product of

What the numbers say:

- The failure is the block period. On the 5 non-periodic quilts the fit ranks the true count
  first in 100 of 100 trials at 2 px. On the 6 periodic quilts 70 of its 73 wrong top-1s are
  harmonics, mostly block counts: lacma-54904 reads 10 x 10 for 50 x 50 squares,
  smithsonian-saam-2017-24-5 reads 5 x 6 for 45 x 54, lacma-63229 reads 11 x 11 for 22 x 22,
  and smithsonian-saam-2017-24-27 reads 7 x 7 for 21 x 21. Block seams are the strongest edges,
  so a block-sized grid scores well on edges at every block size from 2 to 9 squares.
- The seam-energy scorer almost never ranks the true count first on a periodic quilt: 0 at
  every radius on the phone cap (n = 6 at 0 px, 120 otherwise), and at most 2 of 120 on the
  desktop cap. That reproduces exp9 (0 of 37 on image0) on museum photos.
- Tier B: no tier B photo is in the v0 eval set, so n = 0.
- No suggestion at all: the fit methods always suggest; autocorr and the tap sometimes do not
  (the No suggestion column).
- Harmonic alternates as chips: the spike lists a top-1's divisors and its double and triple,
  as square counts. At 0 px the fit's 4 wrong top-1s (the same 4 on each cap and pass) include
  the truth among those alternates on 2 photos (lacma-63229, 22 = 2 x 11;
  smithsonian-saam-2017-24-27, 21 = 3 x 7). The other 2 need a multiple by the squares per
  block (lacma-54904, 50 = 5 x 10; smithsonian-saam-2017-24-5, 45 = 9 x 5 and 54 = 9 x 6),
  which the alternates do not list. The plan's example chips (section 2.1: 40 x 1, 8 x 5 and
  20 x 2) are blocks x squares per block; a chip such as 8 x 5 can recover a block-count top-1
  of 8 only if chips multiply by the squares per block, a wider set than this spike measured.
- Quilter units: across the five methods' 27 correct top-1s at 0 px (phone, proposed-a), the
  block from the repeat matches the proposal's blocks x squares on 9 of 27. Of the 18
  mismatches, 14 find blocks of 2 squares (a repeat whose gcd with the count is 2) on an axis
  the proposals record as one block (13 x 2 for 1 x 26 on lacma-60126, 11 x 2 for 1 x 22 on two
  photos). The other 4 read blocks twice the proposed size, which fits the gcd rule's parity: a
  two-block repeat of 10 squares gives gcd(10, 55) = 5, the right 11 x 5, on a 55-square axis,
  but gcd(10, 60) = 10, a wrong 6 x 10, on a 60-square one (smithsonian-chndm-1973-45-1 down,
  6 x 10 for 12 x 5; lacma-54871, 5 x 10 for 10 x 5 on its 50-square axes). The JSON keeps the
  units, not the repeat, so the repeats named here are inferred. The block period is not
  reliable enough to prefill.

## For other tickets

- B3 (grid-fit check): with exp2's two scores, one global threshold each misses most wrong
  block-count harmonics (26 of 73 rejected at 2 px, phone, proposed-a). The per-trial figure
  (67 of 73) compares each wrong count with the true count's scores, which a check does not
  have, so it is no evidence that a check would work. An idea this spike does not measure:
  compare the confirmed count's two scores with those of its own harmonics on the same photo,
  which needs no truth; its false-alarm rate on true counts would need measuring first, since a
  block count often outscores the true count. G3's simulated miscounts (one square or one
  block off, half and double) also leave out the divisors by 3, 5 and 9 seen here. Both points
  come from a reconstruction of exp2, not its code.
- C3 and B7: no suggestion ships, so nothing to build. The non-periodic result suggests a later
  design where the user types blocks per axis and the engine suggests squares per block; that
  is an observation for Jake, not a scope change. A shipped suggestion would also need a
  confidence in [0, 1] (docs/SPEC.md), and the scores here are unbounded (a wrong top-1's
  product fit reaches 29.68, tap, phone, proposed-b, 4 px); this spike calibrates none.

## Method

Inputs: the v0 eval set (`eval_set` v0 in `corpus/manifest.csv`) of tiers A and B, minus the
holdout (the corpus README's rule, applied through `qrep_eval.annotation.in_holdout` before
any image is read): the 11 tier A dev photos, all museum scans, decoded with cv2 from the main
checkout's private cache. The 2 holdout photos are counted, not named.

For each photo and each proposal pass, with that pass's own field corners and counts:

1. Stage the photo as plan section 2.1 does: crop to the outer frame plus a 5 percent margin at
   full resolution, then cap the long side at 1400 px (phone) or 2000 px (desktop), the caps in
   `web/src/model/downscale.ts`.
2. Move each field corner by the radius (0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 px, plan section 7.2) in its own
   random direction, seeded per photo, pass, cap and radius.
3. Warp the field to a rectangle whose sides are the means of opposite edges.
4. Rank candidate square counts per axis (4 up to extent / 5 px) with each method, then pair
   the axes by rank sum under a square-grid prior (the two pitches within 15 percent; the
   proposed pitches agree within 8 percent). Score the top 3 pairs against the proposed counts.

The methods:

- autocorr: the existing detector's period (a copy of `qrep.vision.grid._find_pitch` at
  8480443, on the gray Sobel projection, as `estimate_grid` builds it); count = extent / pitch,
  then other counts by the autocorrelation at their pitch.
- seam: the mean color edge energy (L*0.5, a, b) on the N - 1 seams a count N implies. A
  reconstruction of exp9 from its public description; the prototype's code is not in the repo.
- fit: candidates ranked by the seam-to-centre edge ratio times a chroma homogeneity gain (the
  within-square chroma mean absolute deviation of squares shifted half a pitch over that of
  aligned squares, so the pitch size cancels). Reconstructed from plan sections 1.2 and B3.
- fit_interior: fit with each square's inner 70 percent standing in for its centre point. Added
  after run 1 to test whether a single centre sample explains the block counts; it changes
  little.
- tap: one tap at a random square's proposed centre, moved by the same radius in the staged
  photo. The 4-connected region of the tapped fabric bounds the pitch (half to twice its size),
  the local window's autocorrelation sets it, and the counts are the nearest integer and its
  two neighbours. A run of 3 or more same-fabric squares puts the true pitch outside that
  bound, so this measures one design of the idea, not its ceiling.
- The fit rule scores each pair over the whole field (`fit_2d`): the ratio, the raw median
  chroma MAD and the product, all three in the JSON per radius-0 trial and as group summaries.

Every knob is a named constant with its source at the top of `scripts/eval/spikes/b1a_counts.py`.
The scoring helpers and the methods have hand-worked tests in `tests/eval/test_b1a_scoring.py`.

## Limits

- n = 11 photos, all museum scans; no phone capture and no screenshot.
- Truth is proposed, not verified (D3b).
- exp2 and exp9 are reconstructions from their public descriptions.
- The crop margin, the candidate floor of 4, the 15 percent prior and the tap's tolerance and

- The harmonic alternates are divisors, double and triple only, and the quilter units come from
  a gcd rule that depends on the count's parity (both under What the numbers say).
- Suggestions carry unbounded scores, not confidences in [0, 1].
